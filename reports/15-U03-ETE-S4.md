## Source defects and cautions

These are defects in the papers, not transcription errors. The converted pages preserve
them.

1. In the 2024-25 paper, Q B3 gives only
   `P(4 < X < 16) >= 24/25` and asks for `E(X)` and `Var(X)`. The usual intended comparison
   with Chebyshev gives centre 10, radius 6, `k = 5`, and variance `36/25`. The displayed
   inequality alone does not determine unique moments, so that answer needs the unstated
   assumption that the interval and bound were constructed directly from Chebyshev.

2. In the same paper, Q B4 prints `phi(1.41) = 0.42, phi(0.19) = 0.50`. The intended normal
   table facts are the mean-to-z areas `phi(1.41) = 0.42` and `phi(0.50) = 0.19`. The second
   ordered pair is reversed in the source.

3. The 2025-26 paper labels the 95 percent confidence-interval value as
   `t_(0.05,9) = 2.262`. Under the common upper-tail notation, 2.262 is `t_(0.025,9)`.
   Q C2 likewise prints `t_(10,0.05) = 2.228`, while 2.228 is the usual two-sided 5 percent
   critical value formed from an upper-tail probability of 0.025. A student should follow
   the supplied numeric values and state the two-sided convention.

4. Summer Q16 asks whether there is enough evidence to "support" the asserted mean of 8.
   A standard null-hypothesis test can reject or fail to reject that assertion; failure to
   reject is not proof that the asserted mean is true.

5. Re-sessional Q B1 gives a variance of `10.8 inches`. Variance has squared units, so this
   should be 10.8 square inches. Q B2 says "20% samples of the population are literate",
   where the intended phrase is "20% of the population". Q C1 calls `f(x)=6x(1-x)` a
   probability distribution function, although it is a probability density function.
